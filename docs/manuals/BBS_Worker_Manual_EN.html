Enter a week and submit eligible drafts

Reference procedure · current controls/source checked; mutations not exercised in this role lab
Go here: Time → Enter a week in a table; Submit this week

The weekly table is a faster entry tool for your own actual daily work. Blank days are skipped. Planned hours remain separate. Review existing rows first so a second table save does not duplicate a day already entered.

Copy previous week layout copies layout with zero hours. It does not assert that last week’s work happened again. Select the destination Week of, choose Copy previous week layout, inspect displayed Previous week and This week, then Add this week’s layout. There are no editable Source/Destination date fields in that flow.

Submit all week drafts handles eligible drafts for your own selected week. A linked correction may appear in the draft count; do not assume weekly submission universally excludes corrections. This lab used individual Submit for the correction so its original link could be inspected. Chief’s own ordinary weekly submission was separately exercised.

Do the task

  1. Open Enter a week in a table; set Week of and Project, then enter actual hours and activities on worked days. Use Save daily drafts.
  1. Open Submit this week. Select the week, read the draft count and inspect each relevant draft before Submit all week drafts.
  1. Refresh the register and inspect resulting states. For a correction, individual detail Submit makes the original/replacement link easy to verify.

Verify the result

  • Draft is distinct from Submitted. A zero-hour copied layout is incomplete until actual work is entered.

If you get stuck

  • A blocked or changed draft must be reviewed through its exact record. Do not repeatedly resubmit the whole week to force a guarded source.

Read your own compensation and reimbursement

Isolated BBS role lab · genuine Worker account · fictional October 2026 records
Go here: My Pay → From / Through → Apply period

My Pay displays your own activity and governed compensation estimates, reviewed settlements, expected dates, actual recorded payments and reimbursement information. Customer invoice frequency and worker payment frequency are independent. Owner/Finance choose settlement periods and expected payment dates; there is no automatic worker payroll cadence demonstrated here.

Worker 1’s historical monthly settlement is USD 720, scheduled November 3, actual paid zero. Daily compensation can differ from actual hours and customer billing units. Worker 2’s October 15–16 approved 195 minutes shows a USD 900 Fixed Per Billing Period estimate; it is not an hourly prorated amount or proof that USD 900 was paid.

Exact activity is 2.5 h + 0.75 h = 3.25 h. My Pay summary rounds to one decimal (3.3 h and 0.8 h travel); rounding is not extra work. “No reviewed payments outstanding” can coexist with an estimate that has not yet become a reviewed settlement. A recorded payment is a business entry, not independent proof of a bank transfer.

Do the task

  1. Select From and Through, then Apply period. Read approved and pending amounts separately by currency.
  1. Open the compensation statement/activity detail and compare exact time records. Read reviewed settlement period, scheduled date, paid and remaining balances independently.
  1. Check reimbursement obligations separately from compensation and customer charges.

Verify the result

  • You see your own pay, not another worker’s or customer invoice economics. Selected source dates may differ from a settlement’s wider period.

If you get stuck

  • Ask Finance about missing rules, period coverage, reimbursement classification, payment references or discrepancies. Do not infer worker pay from a customer invoice or a planned date.
